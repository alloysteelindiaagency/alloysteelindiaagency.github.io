Plastic Mould Steel Comparison

2311, 2738, 2738HH and 2083 all start from a similar pre-hardened mould-steel base, but differ in hardenability, polish, wear resistance and corrosion resistance. Use this table to narrow down which grade fits your tooling job — or use the Steel Grade Finder for a guided recommendation.

GradeCategoryTypical HardnessThrough-HardnessPolishabilityCorrosion ResistanceBest For
2311Plastic mould steel30–34 HRCGood in small/medium sectionsGoodStandardGeneral-purpose plastic injection moulds
2738Plastic mould steel (Ni-alloyed)30–34 HRCExcellent — even in large blocksExcellentStandardLarge, high-polish mould cavities
2738HHPlastic mould steel (Ni-alloyed, high hardness)38–42 HRCExcellent — even in large blocksVery goodStandardTougher, longer-run mould tooling
2083Stainless mould steel280–320 HB (annealed) / 50–54 HRC (hardened)GoodExcellent (mirror-grade)High (~13% Cr)Medical, optical & food-contact tooling

Choose 2311 if…

You need a reliable, cost-effective mould steel for a standard-size cavity and don't have an unusual polish, wear or corrosion requirement. This is our most-ordered grade for a reason.

Choose 2738 if…

Your mould block is large and you need even hardness right through the cross-section, with a consistently high polish across the whole cavity surface.

Choose 2738HH if…

You're running high production volumes or moulding abrasive/glass-filled plastics, where the standard 2738 hardness wouldn't hold up as long.

Choose 2083 if…

Your cavity needs to resist corrosion or humidity — medical devices, optical lenses, or plastics like PVC that attack standard mould steel.
